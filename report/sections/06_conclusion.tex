\section{Conclusion}

We built a question-answering system for SEC filings in which the LLM only interprets the question
and phrases the answer, while SQL, search and code find every number, compute every change and check
every citation. A Spark pipeline on one AWS machine loaded the figures and 10-Q text of every SEC filer
since 2019 into one database: 9,695 companies, 140,436 10-Qs, 3.0 million figures and 4.55 million
passages. New filings are added in minutes.

On quarterly numbers the workflow is far more accurate than simple RAG (96\% against 69\% with Claude
Haiku 5.5, identical over three runs), and the gap is largest for small local models (84\% against
38\% with Qwen2.5-7B). On FinanceBench it is ahead of simple RAG with Haiku (69\% against 54\%), and
always providing the financial statements raises this to 75\%; small models, however, struggle with
the structured answers of the document path. Without retrieval Haiku and Qwen mostly fail. A much
larger model answers many questions from memory, but without sources and without knowledge of new
filings. On the cloud side, our own code takes about 10 ms per question, the Spark jobs scale to the
physical cores of the machine, and the LLM and the SEC download limit are the real bottlenecks.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.} About 27\% of 10-Qs have no MD\&A heading that our section
detector recognises, so they have no text. Quarters are calendar quarters, which differs from the
fiscal quarters of some companies. Text passages from 10-Q web pages leave out tables, so ratio
questions on bulk 10-Qs usually decline. The document path checks that the inputs of a calculation are
printed in the filing, but not that they are the right lines. The free-text grades come from an LLM
judge that was checked against a stronger model but not by a person. Future work could add a human
check of the grades, a load test of the deployed service with many users, a simpler answer format for
small models, and tables in the 10-Q passages.
